# DEC-0219 — Canonical documentation ownership and preserved history

**Date:** 2026-10-08. **Status:** accepted. **Authority:** Josh explicitly requested consolidation,
strict structure enforcement, and permission to archive outdated Markdown.

## Context and choice

Large briefing, architecture, agenda and decision documents mixed current policy, plans, dated
observations and completed session history. Repeated deployment facts and duplicate briefings could
drift. Keep one canonical assistant briefing, a short architecture index with focused topic owners,
a stable-ID active agenda, dated decision records, one observed-environment inventory, operational
runbooks and one readiness ledger. Old paths remain valid through forwarders where needed.

Preserve original snapshots byte-for-byte, all unchecked tasks in a visible revalidation registry,
and all decision reasoning. An old unchecked item is not automatically a current defect; neither
is it silently closed. Preserve existing D1–D4, release safety and healthcare/data boundaries.

## Alternatives and consequences

A single giant document would recreate mixed authority and make review harder. Deleting old records
would lose evidence and unfinished work. Keeping two independent briefing files would permit drift.
Use registered owners, forwarding links, explicit supersession and checked historical captures.
Structural gates cannot understand every prose contradiction; owner review remains required.

## Evidence and implementation owners

[Governance](../../documentation-governance.md), [migration inventory](../../documentation-migration.md),
[manifest](../../documentation-index.json), and `scripts/Test-DocumentationStructure.ps1` with its
negative-case checks define and enforce the structure. CI and release preflight run the gates.

**Supersedes:** no product/operating decision; organization and canonical ownership only.
