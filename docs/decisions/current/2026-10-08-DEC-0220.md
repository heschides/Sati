# DEC-0220 — Evidence-based release readiness thermometers

**Date:** 2026-10-08. **Status:** accepted. **Authority:** Josh requested release-by-release
readiness thermometers and selected separate multitenancy/idempotency scores, overall readiness
and hard blockers. He requested plain descriptions of current protection and future work.

## Context and choice

Settings Release displays Agency separation, Safe repeat requests, and Overall readiness. Overall
includes operations and recovery. Each release embeds its exact reviewed evidence snapshot,
current explanation, next priorities, known problems, all open launch checks, and prior history.
No earlier scores are fabricated. The first record is an explicitly retrospective 1.3.37 baseline.

Use fixed evidence stages and criterion weights, require complete coverage, preserve immutable
versioned rubrics and snapshots, and compare numeric movement only under the same rubric.
Show unchanged progress and regressions honestly. Missing, malformed or mismatched evidence shows
unavailable; a high average cannot waive a blocker. The report is an evidence index, not a defect
percentage, delivery prediction, legal certification, or deployment authorization.

## Alternatives and consequences

Version numbers, test counts, checked tasks and hand-entered optimistic scores are insufficient
proof. A single average could conceal a severe failure. Separate thermometers and explicit launch
checks make the reason for unfinished readiness visible without exposing technical internals.

HTTP, database constraints, durable request identities, transactions, outbox/inbox processing and
provider reconciliation have explicit guarantees and limits. Choosing a newer header or broker
does not create universal exactly-once external effects. Follow the versioned protocol baseline
and authoritative standards crosswalk; review new risks whenever material boundaries change.
