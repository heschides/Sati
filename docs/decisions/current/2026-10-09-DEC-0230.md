# DEC-0230 — Original-claim history, rejection and queued-reservation policy

**Date:** 2026-10-09. **Status:** accepted product policy for future SATI-BIL-001 R1 implementation.
**Authority:** Josh explicitly answered “Adopt all three recommended rules” to the P1–P3 policy
question during the next-item review. This decision records that answer; it is not evidence that
the guard exists, payer certification, DATT, migration or transport activation.

## Context and choice

Generation keys identify requests and retained files, not permission to bill the same service
again. The inspected original export paths can generate/replay files without classifying the
claim's physical history. The proposed guard must preserve harmless generation and exact result
replay while refusing another raw original when delivery is possible or established.

Adopt P1: within one database, include physical/uncertain history for the same trusted agency and
business claim across test/mock modes, accounts and namespaces. Preserve each mode/source as
provenance. IsTest is not a lifecycle bypass. Demo and the personal working environment remain
separate; this policy authorizes no cross-database access or transfer.

Adopt P2: generic upload Rejected/TransportFailed does not establish nonreceipt. Hold for exact
supported receipt/correction evidence or an audited definitive nonreceipt finding. A received
999 rejection and an all-R accepted upload are different facts from absence. The current manual
nonreceipt route supports uncertain states, so extending recovery for generic Rejected requires
a separately scoped action; this decision does not silently broaden it.

Adopt P3: a Queued original reserves the claim against another fresh original generation or
competing queue. Exact retained-result/intent replay remains supported; generated-only history
without an intent remains eligible. Sending/OutcomeUnknown, confirmed receipt and exact matched
receipt retain the proposed stronger holds, including late receipt precedence over nonreceipt.

The [canonical billing design](../../../CLAIMMD_SANDBOX_RUNBOOK.md#proposed-original-release-guard--october-9-2026)
owns the complete fact matrix, mapping, correction boundaries, admission points and acceptance.
`ClaimCorrectionRules` remains authoritative for explicit Resubmit/replacement/void actions;
frequency 1 alone does not distinguish an original from a linked Resubmit. No new adjudication,
billability, correction action or void-purpose rule follows from this choice.

## Rejected alternatives and consequences

Excluding test/mock/account history would create a local bypass for the same business claim.
A genuinely independent certified lane would need an explicit durable lane identity and evidence
boundary; the current mode flag does not supply it. Treating a generic connector rejection as
nonreceipt invents an unsupported transport contract. Allowing extra downloadable originals while
one is queued weakens the reservation and creates another route outside the reserved lifecycle.
Those alternatives are not selected. Neither downloaded bytes nor accepted software tests establish
that an external user/provider refrains from an unrecorded manual submission.

Exact authorized replay returns the retained result, not another physical send. ConfirmedNotReceived
may permit a separate generation only while no contradictory receipt/uncertainty/reservation exists.
Malformed physical evidence cannot disappear from the projection. Independent synthetic positive
tests require independent legitimate histories, rather than an IsTest exemption.

## Technical review and evidence limits

The common SQL admission boundary remains **proposed and unexecuted**. The October 9 review found
Key Vault wrapping inside current SQL transactions and direct poller transaction owners without
a visible single-attempt scope. Encryption preparation, no-op/replay behavior, execution ownership
and lock ordering must be resolved and tested before adopting that boundary across all writers.
The decision does not claim an atomic database-and-network delivery guarantee.
